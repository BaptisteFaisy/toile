Live guest

A QNX 8.0 guest is really running right now, on the pc host. Inside it, the Toile driver enumerates the virtio-gpu, publishes /dev/gpu0, and three ordinary clients paint three bands of the same scanout at ~30 frames per second. This page reads that guest live: the screen is a continuous stream, the console below is the real boot log, and the buttons send real keystrokes into the guest.

What you are looking at

  • The moving plasma is painted by ordinary clients inside the guest (guest/common/toile_anim.c): each opens /dev/gpu0, maps the framebuffer, writes its own band of pixels, and pushes the frame with TRANSFER_TO_HOST_2D + RESOURCE_FLUSH. QEMU renders it, and this page re-captures it through the QEMU monitor (screendump).
  • Three processes (toile-anim --zone 0|1|2 --zones 3) paint three bands of the same scanout, each presenting only its own rectangle: the wall of three clients. The frame counter and the FPS are drawn by the guest — if the guest stopped painting, they would freeze.
  • The buttons send real keystrokes (sendkey on the QEMU monitor → virtio-input → guest/input/toile_input.c → DCMD_TOILE_GPU_ANIM_SET). The keyboard driver owns the shared animation state; the painters only read it, so every client reacts to the same key.
  • The console shows the real boot sequence: TOILE: virtio-gpu trouve (1af4/1050), TOILE: /dev/gpu0 pret, CLIENT: /dev/gpu0 ouvert (fd=3), then ANIM: 151 images, 30.2 im/s, present_ko=0.
  • Proof C: the guest publishes the FNV-1a hash of its own framebuffer and holds the frame; the host recomputes the hash from the pixels QEMU rendered — identical hashes prove the image you see is the image the guest painted.
